What the appeal letter contains

The application was refused as “manifestly unfounded”, with one week to appeal. The letter takes the IND decision apart point by point. Six of the strongest:

  • The perpetrator did not stay in prison. The IND said the state would protect the applicant because the perpetrator went to prison. The letter reads the Turkish court ruling line by line and shows in a dated table that the perpetrator was released 77 days after his arrest. When the judgment came, he had long been free, and the pronouncement of the sentence was deferred. That is not effective, lasting protection.
  • A contradiction inside the decision. The IND left credibility unassessed, yet in the same decision called the fear “not credible”. When credibility is left open, every statement is taken as true; the letter lists, one by one and with their source, the 14 facts the IND had to proceed from.
  • An answer cut short. The IND wrote that the applicant accepted the state could protect him. The letter quotes the full answer from the interview report: the IND had cut the sentence before the word “but”; what followed was revenge by the perpetrator's friends.
  • A low recognition rate. The IND relied on 20 percent or fewer of applicants from Turkey receiving protection. The list it relied on was not attached to the decision, and nobody examined whether that rate represents the applicant's situation. The letter names the category plainly: a witness threatened with death by an influential family.
  • Documents nobody waited for. The IND asked for the documents to be sent through the lawyer, then decided two days later while the sworn translations were on their way. The letter sets the dates side by side: in that case a document missing from the file cannot be held against the applicant.
  • If the label falls, the sanctions fall. The duty to leave the country at once and the two-year entry ban rest only on the “manifestly unfounded” label. If the label falls, both fall and the normal four-week period applies. A request for an interim measure was prepared at the same time, so the applicant can await the ruling in the Netherlands.
