European Union: common rules since 12 June 2026

The EU Pact on Migration and Asylum has applied since 12 June 2026. The right to appeal is now written in two EU regulations:

  • The right to appeal to a court against a negative asylum decision; the court fully examines the facts and the law (APR art. 67). (EU) 2024/1348 (APR)
  • Whether you may stay in the country while the appeal runs (APR art. 68). In some cases staying is not automatic and must be asked of the court separately. (EU) 2024/1348 (APR)
  • Appeal against a decision to send you to another EU country (transfer) (AMMR art. 43). (EU) 2024/1351 (AMMR)

Each country sets the appeal deadline and the court in its own law.

Example: the Netherlands

In the Netherlands the rules for an asylum appeal are in the Aliens Act 2000 (Vreemdelingenwet, Vw) and the General Administrative Law Act (Awb). Each row links to the official text.

General deadline
The general deadline for an appeal to the court (beroep) is 4 weeks. Vw 2000 art. 69
1 week
If the application was not taken up, was found inadmissible (niet-ontvankelijk) or manifestly unfounded (kennelijk ongegrond), or was set aside. Vw 2000 art. 69
2 weeks
If the application was found unfounded (ongegrond), your residence permit was withdrawn, or you were given subsidiary protection (subsidiaire bescherming) instead of refugee status. Vw 2000 art. 69
Transfer decision
The deadline to appeal a decision to send you to another EU country (overdrachtsbesluit) is 1 week. Vw 2000 art. 69
Applications made before 12 June 2026
Under the transitional rule, the terms of the old law apply to these applications. The general term is 4 weeks. It is 1 week for a rejection given within the days of the general asylum procedure (algemene asielprocedure), if the application was not taken up, was found inadmissible or manifestly unfounded, or was set aside, and for transfer decisions. The further appeal (hoger beroep) has the same term. Stb. 2026, 127, art. IX Vw 2000 art. 69 (t/m 11-06-2026) Awb art. 6:24
Staying in the country
As a rule the decision is not carried out until the appeal deadline ends or the appeal is decided. In the cases the EU regulation lists (APR art. 68(3)) and in transfer decisions this protection is not automatic. Vw 2000 art. 82 (EU) 2024/1348 (APR)
Interim measure
In those cases you ask the court for an interim measure (voorlopige voorziening) to stay: within 1 week of notification for a refusal or withdrawal, together with the appeal for a transfer decision. Until the interim-measure deadline has passed, and if you asked in time until the court decides on your request, you are not removed from the Netherlands; for a transfer decision this applies to the first request. For a subsequent application this protection does not apply in two cases (the principle of non-refoulement still applies): if the appeal is considered to be lodged merely to delay or prevent an imminent removal, and in the situations of APR art. 56(a) and (b). Vw 2000 art. 69 Awb art. 8:81 Vb 2000 art. 7.3
Court fee
No court fee is charged for an asylum appeal or for the request for an interim measure. Vw 2000 art. 81
What the court examines
The court fully examines the facts and the law as they stand on the day it decides (ex nunc). Vw 2000 art. 83a (EU) 2024/1348 (APR)
Higher court
The deadline for a further appeal (hoger beroep) to the Raad van State: 2 weeks in 1-week cases, 4 weeks in 2-week cases. The letter must state the grounds against the first court's ruling (grieven); without them the further appeal is inadmissible and the gap cannot be repaired later. Vw 2000 art. 69 Vw 2000 art. 85
How the deadline is counted
The deadline starts the day after the decision was notified. The letter must reach the court before the last day ends; a letter sent by post counts as on time if it is posted before the last day ends and arrives within 1 week after. Awb art. 6:8 Awb art. 6:9
If you are late
If you can reasonably not be blamed for the delay, the appeal is not rejected for it. The reason must be written clearly in the letter. Awb art. 6:11
What the letter must contain
Signature, name and address, date, a description of the decision appealed and the grounds; a copy of the decision if possible. If needed, a translation of a letter in a foreign language is added. Awb art. 6:5

Verified court rulings

We read each ruling in full in the official case-law database. Our appeal letters use only rulings read this way.

Raad van State · July 6, 2026
A further appeal was filed without grounds and more time was asked to give them (the case was about a return decision). Raad van State: the law has no such extra time; without grounds the appeal is inadmissible. So we write the full grounds in the first letter. ECLI:NL:RVS:2026:3841
Raad van State · July 15, 2026
In a planning case the Raad van State had the impression the appeal was written with an AI such as ChatGPT: the case numbers it cited were about other subjects. So we read every case number we put in a letter, one by one, in the official text. ECLI:NL:RVS:2026:4143
Rechtbank Den Haag · October 7, 2026
The switch to the two-status system on 12 June 2026 came without transitional rules. The court excused the late appeal of a person with subsidiary protection (verschoonbaar) and upheld the appeal. ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2026:29518
Rechtbank Den Haag · August 28, 2026
The IND, which decides on behalf of the Minister for Asylum and Migration, did not decide an asylum application from 2024 in time. The court ordered a decision within 6 weeks and €50 for each day of delay (at most €15,000). The end of the ruling states the further-appeal deadline: 4 weeks after the ruling was sent. We always take the deadline from this text in the ruling itself. ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2026:24869

How we work

  • We take the deadline from the appeal information at the end of your decision, compare it with the country's law and set a safe last day.
  • We read every law article and court ruling we put in a letter at the official source; we never use a case number we have not read.
  • We write the full grounds in the first letter; we do not count on getting extra time later.
  • We write the letter in the court's language and explain what it says to you in your own language.
